Which Method? A Flowchart

Every problem in this book answers the same three questions:

  1. Which parameter? A mean, a proportion or a variance.
  2. How many groups? One, two, or three or more.
  3. What is special about the data? Is \(\sigma\) known? Are the samples paired? Are the variances equal? Are there blocks?

Which Method? Practice

For each study, name the parameter(s), the method and the section.

  1. The Bank of Thailand wants to know whether the share of households with credit-card debt has risen above 35%.
  2. A researcher compares the mean wage of 40 workers in Chiang Mai with the mean wage of 35 workers in Khon Kaen.
  3. The same 25 firms report their profits before and after a tax change.
  4. An analyst asks whether the daily returns of a stock index have become more volatile than a standard deviation of 1.2%.
  5. Four fertilizer subsidy schemes are each tried in five districts, with the scheme randomly assigned to villages within each district.
  6. A survey compares the proportion of men and of women who own a business.

Solution: Which Method? Practice

  1. One proportion, \(z\) test for \(p\) (2.3), \(H_1: p > 0.35\).

  2. Two independent means: \(F\) test for the variances first, then pooled or Welch \(t\) (3.4, 3.5).

  3. Paired means: paired \(t\) test on the differences (3.3).

  4. One variance: chi-square test, \(H_1: \sigma^2 > 1.44\) (2.4).

  5. Four means with blocks (districts): RCBD ANOVA (4.3), then Tukey if \(H_0\) is rejected.

  6. Two proportions: \(z\) test for \(p_1 - p_2\) (3.1).

Mixed Problems

All figures are hypothetical. Use \(\alpha\) = 0.05 and the five steps.

M1. In a survey of 500 workers, 210 have a second job. Test whether more than 40% of workers have a second job. Use the critical region.

Solution: M1

One proportion (2.3). \(\hat{p} = 210/500 = 0.42\).

Step 1. \(H_0: p = 0.40 \quad H_1: p > 0.40\)

Step 2. \(\alpha\) = 0.05

Step 3. \(\sqrt{0.40(0.60)/500} = 0.0219\); \(z_{\text{cal}} = (0.42 - 0.40)/0.0219 = 0.913\)

Step 4. Critical region: \(z_{\text{cal}} > 1.645\).

Step 5. \(z_{\text{cal}} = 0.913\) does not fall in the critical region (\(0.913 < 1.645\)), so we accept \(H_0\). That is, the proportion of workers with a second job is equal to 0.40 at the 0.05 level of significance.

M2. The monthly profit (thousand baht) of 9 cafés in the old city has \(\bar{x}\) = 52.4, \(s\) = 6.1; that of 13 cafés near the university has \(\bar{x}\) = 47.8, \(s\) = 5.4. (a) Use the \(F\) test with the critical region to check the variances. (b) Test whether the mean profits are different, using the critical region.

Solution: M2

(a) \(H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2\) vs \(H_1: \sigma_1^2 \ne \sigma_2^2\). \(F_{\text{cal}} = 37.21/29.16 = 1.276\), df = (8, 12). Critical region: \(F_{\text{cal}} < 1/F_{0.975,\,12,\,8} = 0.2381\) or \(F_{\text{cal}} > F_{0.975,\,8,\,12} = 3.51\). \(F_{\text{cal}}\) does not fall in the critical region, so we accept \(H_0\): the variances are equal. Use the pooled \(t\) test.

(b)

Step 1. \(H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 \quad H_1: \mu_1 \ne \mu_2\)

Step 2. \(\alpha\) = 0.05

Step 3. \(s_p^2 = [8(37.21) + 12(29.16)]/20 = 32.38\); SE \(= \sqrt{32.38(1/9 + 1/13)} = 2.4675\); \(t_{\text{cal}} = (52.4 - 47.8)/2.4675 = 1.864\), df = 20

Step 4. Critical region: \(t_{\text{cal}} < -2.086\) or \(t_{\text{cal}} > 2.086\).

Step 5. \(t_{\text{cal}} = 1.864\) does not fall in the critical region (\(-2.086 < 1.864 < 2.086\)), so we accept \(H_0\). That is, the mean monthly profits of cafés in the two areas are equal at the 0.05 level of significance.

M3. A central bank wants the standard deviation of the monthly inflation rate to be less than 1 percentage point. Over 15 months, \(s\) = 0.8. Assume normality and test whether the target is met, using the critical region.

Solution: M3

One variance (2.4).

Step 1. \(H_0: \sigma^2 = 1 \quad H_1: \sigma^2 < 1\)

Step 2. \(\alpha\) = 0.05

Step 3. \(\chi^2_{\text{cal}} = 14(0.64)/1 = 8.960\), df = 14

Step 4. \(\chi^2_{0.05,\,14} = 6.57\). Critical region: \(\chi^2_{\text{cal}} < 6.57\).

Step 5. \(\chi^2_{\text{cal}} = 8.960\) does not fall in the critical region (\(8.960 > 6.57\)), so we accept \(H_0\). That is, the standard deviation of monthly inflation is equal to 1 percentage point at the 0.05 level of significance; the data do not show that the target is met.

M6. The mean household size in a district is estimated from 20 households: \(\bar{x}\) = 3.42, \(s\) = 0.58. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the mean household size.

Solution: M6

One mean, \(\sigma\) unknown (2.2). \(t_{0.975,\,19} = 2.093\), \(s/\sqrt{n} = 0.1297\); \(3.42 \pm 2.093(0.1297) = 3.42 \pm 0.2715\). We are 95% confident that the mean household size is between 3.15 and 3.69 persons.

Writing a Full Conclusion

A full conclusion has a decision and an interpretation in the words of the problem:

Decision. [statistic or p-value] … so we [reject / accept] \(H_0\). Interpretation. That is, … at the 0.05 level of significance.

Checklist:

  • If you reject \(H_0\), write \(H_1\) as a sentence; if you accept \(H_0\), write \(H_0\) as a sentence.
  • Name the population and the variable (not just “the mean”).
  • For a two-sided test that rejects \(H_0\), add the direction from the sample statistics.
  • For ANOVA, “not all equal”; then use Tukey to say which ones differ.

Write the decision and the interpretation for each result.

  1. \(H_0: \mu = 15000\), \(H_1: \mu \ne 15000\) for the mean monthly rent (baht) in a district; \(t_{\text{cal}} = -2.514\), critical region \(|t_{\text{cal}}| > 2.064\), \(\bar{x}\) = 13,850.
  2. \(H_0: p_1 = p_2\), \(H_1: p_1 > p_2\) for the proportions of urban (1) and rural (2) households using mobile banking; p-value = 0.0832.
  3. One-way ANOVA comparing mean loan sizes at three types of lender; p-value = 0.0007.

Solution: Writing a Full Conclusion

  1. \(t_{\text{cal}} = -2.514\) falls in the critical region (\(-2.514 < -2.064\)), so we reject \(H_0\). That is, the mean monthly rent in the district is different from 15,000 baht at the 0.05 level of significance; since \(\bar{x}\) = 13,850, it is lower than 15,000 baht.

  2. p-value = 0.0832 \(\ge\) \(\alpha\) = 0.05, so we accept \(H_0\). That is, the proportions of urban and rural households using mobile banking are equal at the 0.05 level of significance.

  3. p-value = 0.0007 < \(\alpha\) = 0.05, so we reject \(H_0\). That is, the mean loan sizes are not all equal across the three types of lender at the 0.05 level of significance. (Use Tukey’s method to find which types differ.)
